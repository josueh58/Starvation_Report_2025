Walleye exhibited an overall fair body condition in 2025, with a mean relative weight (Wr) of 86.2. The sample encompassed a wide range of length and age classes, with a particularly notable abundance of individuals within the 250–350 mm total length range. Larger Walleye were present in the sample, although their representation was reduced compared to FWIN 2024, particularly in the preferred and memorable PSD classes.

Sex ratio analysis revealed 96 males, 37 females, and 82 unidentified individuals. Males were most prevalent in the smaller length classes, suggesting the presence of a strong reproductive cohort. Conversely, females were more frequently observed in the larger size classes, consistent with expected sexual dimorphism in growth. The high number of unsexed individuals is likely attributable to the sampling period, as many fish were not yet exhibiting fully developed gonads. 

Walleye relative abundance increased to 43% of the total catch in 2025,  with a CPUE of 1.12 fish per hour—the highest recorded CPUE for Walleye since FWIN sampling began at Starvation Reservoir. This increase may be partially explained by the lower reservoir elevation observed during sampling; the lake was measured at 62.5% of full pool, which may have likely contributed to increased fish concentration and capture efficiency.

A breakdown of relative weights across PSD size classes revealed distinct patterns. While Quality (87.4), Preferred (91.5), and Memorable (92.7) classes demonstrated fair to good condition, the Sub-stock (85.0) and particularly Stock-sized individuals (79.8) displayed poor condition factors, indicative of density-dependent stress. These trends suggest that intraspecific competition among juvenile and stock-sized fish may be limiting growth and forage availability, resulting in suppressed body condition.
